1 · BAHRAIN — SATURDAY NIGHT LEAD STORY · THE GULF WAR SPILLS INTO A NEW STATE

Iran strikes US bases in Bahrain

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said they hit two US military positions in Bahrain late on Saturday with missiles and drones, claiming they destroyed two Patriot air-defence radars — the first strike on the Gulf kingdom that hosts the US Navy’s Fifth Fleet.

Bahrain said the attack struck a civilian industrial facility and started a fire; no injuries were reported, and its air defences intercepted other attacks. Which bases were hit is disputed: the Guards named al-Riffa and Awali, Fars named Sheikh Isa and Juffair. Tehran calls it retaliation for US drones in Iranian airspace.

2
WARSAW · POLAND

Rutte in Warsaw: NATO will defend “every inch”

A day after two Russian missiles and a suspected drone came down in Poland, NATO chief Mark Rutte guaranteed Poland’s defence in Warsaw. Poland wants Eastern Sentry reinforcements — early-warning aircraft and F-35s; NATO’s council meets Monday.

3
CAMBRIDGE · SCIENCE

After 167 years, the Riemann hypothesis falls — to an AI

Independent mathematicians verified the Lean formal proof on Saturday: Anthropic’s Levent Alpöge and the Claude AI claim maths’ most famous open problem, posed in 1859. A rival human proof emerged the same day; the $1m Clay prize is at stake.

4
KRYVYI RIH · UKRAINE

“Triple-tap” strike kills a boy and a paramedic

Two ballistic missiles hit the centre of Zelenskyy’s hometown on Friday evening; drones then hit the rescuers. A 7-year-old boy and a 20-year-old paramedic were killed, 58–60 people wounded, six of them children.

5
GUASAVE · MEXICO

Mexico captures FBI most-wanted “El Chapo Isidro”

Special forces seized Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, on the FBI’s Ten Most Wanted list with a $5m reward, on Friday night. Retaliatory violence then killed at least 11 — four soldiers and seven gunmen.

6
ANTALYA · CLIMATE

COP31 stalls: fossil fuels vanish from the draft as talks run past deadline

The UN climate summit ran deep into overtime on Saturday with no deal, after a draft dropped all mention of fossil fuels and a proposed $120bn adaptation target. “It’s either going to be a good deal or it’s going to be no deal,” said EU climate chief Wopke Hoekstra. Nearly 200 countries remain divided, some decisions may slip to 2027, and the US sent no official delegation.
